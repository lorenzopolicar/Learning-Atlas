# C004 — Retrieval practice supports delayed retention and some transfer

## Evidence and reasoning

Controlled experiments show that retrieval can outperform restudy on delayed tests even when restudy looks better immediately [S006]. A meta-analysis across 192 effects reports positive transfer overall with meaningful moderators [S007].

## Boundary conditions

Near transfer is better supported than far transfer. Retrieval should be appropriately difficult, psychologically safe, and followed by useful feedback when errors would otherwise persist.

## Product relevance

Ask the learner to recall, explain, predict, or produce before revealing an answer. Schedule later independent checks and distinguish retrieval for learning from surveillance or grading.
