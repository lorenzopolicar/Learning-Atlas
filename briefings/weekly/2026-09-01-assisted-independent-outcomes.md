# Assisted versus independent outcomes — 2026-09-01

## Signal

Current controlled evidence does not support a single “GenAI effect on learning.” The more useful split is between the support policy, the effort and workflow it changes, and what the learner can later do after substantive support is removed. Three newly inspected studies add a null/inconclusive one-week contrast, a fragile positive timing intervention, and an authentic clinical-performance null [S023, S024, S025].

## What changed

- Added [S023]: Copilot made novice/intermediate programmers substantially faster with less reported effort, but did not yield a reliable absolute one-week independent advantage over tasks first completed with a human peer.
- Added [S024] and low-confidence [C011]: delayed access beat always-on access on a same-session unassisted test and confidence accuracy, but non-random workshop assignment, exclusions, imbalance, clustering and policy bundling prevent a strong causal claim.
- Added [S025] and [C012]: access to GPT-4o after brief training did not improve authentic clinical-assessment performance across four South African hospitals, even though most learners perceived it as helpful.
- Updated [C001], [C002], [P001] and living review [R001] without increasing their maturity.
- Held two highly relevant studies instead of admitting them from abstracts: the 275-student Bassner et al. programming null RCT and Barcauí's randomized 45-day retention study. Both remain in screening until lawful full methods are inspectable.

## Interpretation

The programming studies expose a measurement trap. A large assisted-to-unassisted drop can arise because assistance inflates the starting score; it is not automatically proof that absolute learning was harmed. Report the assisted outcome, the later absolute outcome and the within-condition change separately.

The access-timing result makes progressive assistance more testable, but it does not license an RL gate. The next product-relevant comparison is simpler: always on versus a transparent attempt threshold versus no substantive assistance, with access supports held constant and a delayed independent/transfer outcome.

The clinical trial adds a different warning. General tool availability, usage amount and a short briefing do not constitute human–AI collaborative capability. Workflow integration, task switching, verification and domain knowledge matter, especially in authentic high-load settings.

## Product implication

For Orqestra, preserve three distinct records:

1. what happened while support was available;
2. what the learner could do later against an accessible independent baseline;
3. how the learner framed, checked, repaired or escalated AI assistance.

Do not turn the difference between the first two into a universal dependence score. Test whether a declared support policy improves the second and whether workflow evidence adds prediction beyond domain accuracy.

## Portfolio effect

This pass adds a small US programming study, a multilingual European university laboratory study and a multicentre South African medical trial. It improves contextual variety and adds null/inconclusive evidence. The corpus remains thin on long horizons, younger learners outside a few contexts, non-English publications, disabled-learner outcomes under controlled interventions, and far transfer.

## Next question

Can a transparent attempt-before-access policy replicate [S024] over a one-week delay and near transfer, and does any adaptive policy outperform that fixed rule without adding gaming, inequity or opacity?
