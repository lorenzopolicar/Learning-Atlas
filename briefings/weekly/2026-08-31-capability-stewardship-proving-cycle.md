# Capability stewardship proving cycle — 2026-08-31

## Signal

The Atlas should not turn **capability sovereignty** into a learner score. The phrase is useful as a normative question about authorship, contestability, support and effective control, but it bundles several existing constructs. For operational work, use the narrower phrase **AI-mediated task stewardship** and preserve a multidimensional evidence portfolio [N001, D002].

## What changed

- Added [C010]: in bounded tasks with known correctness and recorded pre-advice judgments, correct adoption and correct resistance are distinct; raw confidence is not enough.
- Revised [C008] and [B004] so independent capability is defined against an accessible baseline and assistance is described by function, not mere tool presence.
- Added three fully inspected sources: a controlled reliance experiment [S020], a formal/empirical model of metacognitive sensitivity [S021], and interviews with 33 disabled higher-education students [S022].
- Produced an [Orqestra design brief](../../bridges/orqestra-ai-mediated-stewardship.md), decision [D002], and falsifiable low-stakes experiment [E001].
- Reweighted the research queue so construct validation and pilot co-design precede implementation.

## The practical position

Orqestra should represent three evidence lanes—accessible independent, AI-assisted and supervisory/recovery—and record whether assistance restored access, represented information, supported expression or executive function, delegated target cognition, or mixed these functions.

Where truth is defensible and enough disagreement trials exist, report correct adoption and correct resistance separately. Also observe verification, repair, escalation, delayed accessible-independent performance and transfer. Do not create a `capabilitySovereigntyScore`, overload Bloom level, infer dependence from usage frequency, or treat planted-error trials as general proof of agency.

## Learner-voice correction

[S022] is not effect evidence, but it changes the validity argument. Some disabled learners described AI as reducing pain, increasing autonomy or compensating for inaccessible materials and inadequate accommodations. The same participants voiced overreliance and stopped-learning concerns. A universal no-tool condition would collapse these different functions and may punish the people for whom a tool restores access.

## What remains unsettled

- whether evaluative control is teachable and transfers across domains;
- how much internalized knowledge reliable oversight requires;
- how to assess stewardship where truth is plural, delayed or normative;
- whether the new language adds decision value beyond domain performance, metacognition and self-regulation;
- what responsibilities belong to the learner versus the system and institution;
- whether the required trials and confidence prompts create unacceptable burden.
