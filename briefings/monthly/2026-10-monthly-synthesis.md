## Contradictions and live uncertainty

- Answer-ready GPT-5 improved assisted success and was followed by worse immediate same-family unaided performance in S057. S058 detected no AI-only difference under an explicit-request assistance policy, but its interval was wide (`OR .80`, two-sided `95% CI [.42, 1.51]`). This is a cross-study moderator hypothesis, not evidence that explicit-request friction caused the difference.
- S058's contextual-feedback estimates were favourable under preregistered one-sided tests, while the two-sided intervals touched or crossed the null: offloading `OR .47, CI [.22, 1.00]`; immediate unaided accuracy `OR 1.51, CI [.98, 2.33]`. The bundle was not ablated and neither S057 nor S058 measured delayed learning.
- S051/S052 reported longer-horizon personalization benefits; S053 found no value from a short default-parameter adaptation. The tension concerns evidence accumulation, comparator strength, calibration, coverage and policy scope—not a vote for or against personalization.
- S054–S056 show that access, implementer and enacted service design alter take-up and transfer. They falsify software-only portability, not every possible low-support implementation.
- Critical and capability sources challenge an individual-only account of agency: task stewardship cannot validate educational purpose, subjectification, substantive freedom or institutional legitimacy.
- Removing substantive AI assistance can expose independent capability while also removing access. An accessible-independent baseline must preserve construct-irrelevant access support.

There is still no credible current-system study of delayed novel independent transfer under monitored exposure. Confidence remains unchanged where evidence is immediate, bundled, unreplicated or not yet published to the Atlas.

## Orqestra implications and falsifiable experiments

Do not edit the Orqestra repository from this synthesis. The highest-value next experiment is a current-system, multi-session comparison of:

1. answer-ready assistance;
2. complete answers only on explicit request;
3. contextual or metacognitive feedback;
4. learner-controlled support.

Hold task, time, interface transitions and exposure budget constant. Record assistance tier and actual exposure. Measure delayed novel accessible-independent performance, transfer, subgroup effects, frustration, accessibility burden and delivery cost. Use two-sided inference and predeclare the smallest meaningful effect.

Secondary experiments are:

- fixed versus learner-model versus learner-controlled sequencing, with sufficient warm-up, calibration, content coverage, subgroup error, cost and delayed outcomes;
- software-only versus an explicit institutional support bundle, measuring achieved dose, displaced instruction, failure recovery, teacher workload and authority, subgroup participation, full cost and persistence after de-support;
- E001 governance co-design in which learners, educators and affected communities can change a material decision and have a credible alternative or exit.

Falsify the proposed directions if simple policies match delayed outcomes at lower burden, if support bundles add no implementation value, if subgroup or accessibility harms dominate average gains, or if current-system monitored replications remove the apparent assistance-policy differences.
