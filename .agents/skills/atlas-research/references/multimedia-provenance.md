# Multimedia provenance

## Identity

Record the publisher page, series/event, episode or talk title, hosts and speakers, publication date, duration, GUID/platform ID, media URL when lawful, and the canonical feed or channel.

## Transcript ladder

Prefer, in order:

1. publisher transcript with stable URL;
2. official human-edited captions;
3. official automatic captions;
4. a locally generated transcript from user-authorized media;
5. no transcript, with direct listening and timestamped notes.

Record which rung was used, language, retrieval date, transcription engine/version if applicable, diarization method, content hash for a local working copy, and uncertain spans. A transcript is a navigation surface; audio/video remains the authority for consequential quotations.

## Locator format

Use `HH:MM:SS–HH:MM:SS`, speaker, a paraphrased observation or short lawful excerpt, transcript confidence, and follow-up. Timestamp the publisher media rather than a player-specific elapsed time when possible.

## Epistemic interpretation

- Expert perspective can shape beliefs, design taste, and the research agenda.
- Firsthand accounts can establish what the speaker experienced or intended, within memory and incentive limits.
- Product claims establish what the maker says, not independent efficacy.
- Normative arguments should be steel-manned and compared with counterpositions.
- Empirical statements must resolve to original studies before supporting a claim.

## Rights and storage

Do not commit full transcripts, captions, or media unless the work is clearly licensed for redistribution and doing so is necessary. Store lawful working copies in `.harness/inbox/`, Zotero, Drive, or another approved private store. Git contains original synthesis, bounded excerpts, timestamps, hashes, and source links.
