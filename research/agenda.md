# Research agenda

The atlas pursues a coherent programme rather than an unbounded topic feed. The programme asks how intelligent systems can help people develop, demonstrate, and govern capability in an AI-rich world.

## North-star inquiry

How should an AI-powered learning system represent a person, choose an intervention, preserve agency and productive effort, and produce evidence that the person—not merely the system—has become more capable?

## Programme 1 — Learning versus performance

Determine when AI-assisted success becomes durable independent learning, when it creates dependency or illusion, and which interaction policies change that relationship.

- Anchor: [Q001](../questions/Q001-when-does-ai-performance-become-learning.md)
- Active review: [R001](../reviews/R001-learning-performance-gap/protocol.md)
- Priority outcomes: independent performance, delayed retention, near/far transfer, calibration, agency.

## Programme 2 — Learner and capability modelling

Design a longitudinal, evidence-backed representation of what a person can do independently and with tools, how certain that inference is, and which action it may justify.

- Anchor: [Q002](../questions/Q002-what-should-a-learner-model-represent.md)
- Topics: knowledge tracing, evidence-centred design, competency graphs, temporal decay, uncertainty, privacy, contestability, collaborative capability.

## Programme 3 — Intervention policy

Learn how to select and sequence explanations, questions, examples, retrieval, feedback, reflection, collaboration, practice, and human escalation.

- Topics: expertise reversal, cognitive load, feedback, self-explanation, metacognition, motivation, affect, tutoring dialogue, spacing, interleaving.
- Product question: what is the simplest policy that improves a declared outcome for a defined learner and context?

## Programme 4 — Assessment and learning assurance

Build validity arguments for capability evidence when AI is ubiquitous. Separate independent capability, human-AI capability, and generated output without treating any one as morally superior by default.

- Topics: programme-level assessment, authenticity, process evidence, measurement invariance, fairness, high-stakes governance, identity and authorship.

## Programme 5 — Purposes, power, and the future of learning

Develop a position on what is worth learning, who decides, whose interests systems serve, and what should never be optimized away.

- Anchor: [Q003](../questions/Q003-what-is-worth-learning-in-an-ai-world.md)
- Method: structured discourse, historical cases, stakeholder voices, philosophical argument, and empirical evidence kept visibly distinct.

## Programme 6 — Learning systems in institutions

Study the sociotechnical conditions under which good learning designs survive contact with schools, universities, and workplaces.

- Topics: teacher agency, workflow, governance, incentives, procurement, accessibility, privacy, implementation fidelity, organizational capability, change over time.

## Portfolio rules

- Keep at least one empirical, one architectural, and one normative question active.
- Prefer questions that can change a product principle or invalidate an assumption.
- Balance recent AI research with durable learning science and critical/historical perspectives.
- Do not let the easiest-to-measure population or outcome define the field.
- Each quarterly review may add a programme only by retiring or merging another.
