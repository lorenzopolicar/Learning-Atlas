# Agenda evolution ledger

The research agenda is a versioned hypothesis about which questions will most improve the Learning Atlas and Orqestra—not a permanent table of contents. The six programmes provide continuity. Their priority, active questions, methods, source lanes, and even their continued existence remain revisable.

## Evolution loop

1. **Signals arrive continuously:** new evidence, contradictions, product decisions, experiments, field shifts, institutional needs, stakeholder testimony, neglected populations, and repeated retrieval failures.
2. **Weekly queue shaping:** convert useful signals into bounded questions or attach them to an existing review. Do not reorganize a programme because a topic is fashionable.
3. **Monthly portfolio review:** compare evidence gaps, source-lane balance, unanswered product decisions, and claims most vulnerable to falsification. Reweight queue items explicitly.
4. **Quarterly direction review:** retain, merge, split, add, or retire programmes. Record the old and new agenda, the signals that justified the change, opportunity cost, and the review date.
5. **Annual constitutional review:** revisit the north-star inquiry, epistemic policy, intended beneficiaries, and relationship to Orqestra. This can change the shape of the atlas, but never erase prior reasoning from Git history.

## Agenda proposal contract

Every material agenda change records:

- proposal date and author/agent;
- triggering signals and linked atlas IDs;
- current question or programme affected;
- proposed wording, priority, method, and source lanes;
- why this is more decision-relevant or falsifiable;
- what receives less attention as a consequence;
- bias or novelty risk;
- review horizon and success signal;
- human decision and rationale.

Agents may propose agenda changes in a briefing or pull request. Scheduled agents never rewrite the north star, add a programme, or retire a programme without human review.

### 2026-09-02 — Technology-sensitive recency preference

- Proposal and decision: the user explicitly prefers recent AI research because model capability has advanced materially beyond GPT-4o-era systems.
- Method change: for model-dependent effects, search the rolling 18-month window and newer system generations first; record system/version and study period separately from publication year; expose a freshness register and recurring review date.
- Boundary: do not discard older learning science, measurement theory, causal studies, null results or mechanisms merely because they are old. Classify their technology dependence and make transfer assumptions explicit.
- Product implication: current Orqestra advice must include a current-system evidence anchor or state that transfer from older systems is an untested hypothesis.
- Opportunity cost: some autonomous search capacity shifts from accumulating additional GPT-4/4o-era intervention papers to finding replications with newer systems and comparable independent/delayed outcomes.
- Bias and novelty risk: recent work is more likely to be preprint-only, underpowered, under-replicated or benchmark-driven. Methodological quality and outcome relevance remain separate from freshness.
- Review horizon and success signal: review monthly; success means `atlas.py freshness` exposes no unlabeled model-dependent source and product briefs distinguish current, recent and historical systems.
- Human decision: accepted by direct instruction on 2 September 2026.
