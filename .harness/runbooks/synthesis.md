# Synthesis runbook

## Unit of synthesis

Synthesize by claim, mechanism, boundary, and outcome. Do not summarize a folder paper by paper.

## Procedure

1. Define the target claim and its current wording.
2. Retrieve supporting and contradicting sources; inspect their notes and, where needed, originals.
3. Build an evidence profile across internal validity, directness, consistency, replication, magnitude, duration, transfer, and ecological validity.
4. Explain heterogeneity using learner, task, intervention, comparator, measurement, time, and setting.
5. Write the strongest supportable claim. Prefer narrower high-integrity language over broad low-integrity language.
6. Name counterevidence and live uncertainty.
7. Update beliefs only when the cross-claim reasoning changes.
8. Update principles only when the design implication and falsifier remain explicit.
9. Identify affected decisions and propose experiments.

## Anti-patterns

- vote counting papers;
- treating statistical significance as importance;
- averaging incompatible outcomes;
- converting absence of evidence into evidence of absence;
- “research shows” without IDs and boundaries;
- treating theory, guidance, and causal evidence as interchangeable;
- using engagement as a synonym for learning;
- letting a model resolve a disagreement by rhetoric.
