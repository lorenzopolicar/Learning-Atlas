# R001 seed synthesis

Status: provisional orientation synthesis; not exhaustive.

## What we can say now

Nine comparative studies now make the early picture more heterogeneous. Unrestricted AI assistance can create large supported-performance gains while weakening later unaided performance [S001]. Yet ordinary GPT-4o access also improved immediate and one-week unaided performance in a time-held learning task [S015]. In programming, Copilot created a large supported speed/completion advantage without a reliable absolute advantage when the same learners repeated tasks alone one week later [S023]. A tightly designed AI tutor can improve short-term learning relative to a specific active-learning lesson [S002], and a structured, teacher-supported programme can produce meaningful assessment gains in a low-resource secondary context [S005].

The first direct access-timing study reports that delayed access beat always-on access on an immediate independent test while matching no access [S024], but session assignment, exclusion and clustering problems make this a low-confidence component signal. In an authentic four-hospital South African clinical trial, minimally trained point-of-care GPT-4o access did not improve blinded bedside assessment performance despite high perceived helpfulness [S025]. This is evidence about workflow-bound performance, not a test of a practiced tutor or durable learning.

Two new studies add complementary but fragile boundaries. A 16-week attempt-first, compare-and-verify GPT-4o workflow was associated with higher unfamiliar supervised immediate and delayed engineering-transfer scores than a matched curated-alternative workflow across six Uzbek universities [S063]. Deterministic post-baseline class allocation means this is configuration-level association, not randomized component evidence. In a current GPT-5.3 writing comparison, open collaboration produced the strongest assisted products while bounded support plus compulsory reflection produced the strongest observed immediate no-AI near-transfer profile [S064]. Six class-confounded clusters and boundary-level exact inference prevent a directional harm or reusable-policy claim.

The synthesis is therefore not “AI helps” or “AI harms,” nor is it “guardrails always beat general access.” The more defensible early claims are that assisted output cannot establish learning [C001], general access can produce short-delay learning under bounded conditions [C009], and task, incentive, population, interaction and institutional structure are plausible moderators that require direct tests. No current-system study in the inspected set combines separately scored assisted performance, immediate independent performance and delayed novel independent transfer with verified withdrawal.

## Candidate mechanisms

- answer provision may bypass retrieval, generation, error detection, and self-explanation;
- access timing may preserve an initial problem representation and effort before offloading, but transparent fixed timing has not yet been separated from adaptive machinery;
- progressive hints may preserve some of that cognitive work;
- task structure can focus attention and prevent plausible but pedagogically unhelpful responses;
- teacher facilitation may support prompting, interpretation, motivation, classroom management, and error correction;
- increased time-on-task or novelty may contribute to positive programme results;
- holding time fixed may reveal learning-per-unit-time gains that disappear when learners use AI to reduce total study time;
- learner-selected augmentation and automation may mark different mechanisms, but post-treatment use categories cannot establish their causal effects;
- assessments aligned closely to the intervention may overstate transfer.

These are mechanism hypotheses, not yet established components.

## Product direction

Build and test an explicit assistance policy rather than a monolithic chatbot. Log support state. Evaluate after support is removed. Preserve a legitimate performance-support mode, but do not use its outputs as silent proof of independent capability [P001, P005].

## What we do not know

- durability over months rather than days or weeks;
- far transfer and authentic performance;
- differential effects by prior knowledge, language, disability, motivation, and socioeconomic context;
- which guardrail components cause benefit;
- how learners adapt strategically over repeated use;
- whether a simple attempt threshold replicates the access-timing result and whether any adaptive policy adds value beyond it;
- whether dependence, calibration, and agency can be measured reliably;
- whether the positive one-week effect in [S015] replicates in ordinary coursework, longer horizons and less selective populations;
- whether the positive delayed configuration-level association in [S063] survives prospective randomization with a current frozen model and matched adaptivity;
- whether the current-system pattern in [S064] survives many-cluster randomization that independently varies delegation limits and reflection;
- the publication-bias and null-results landscape;
- final version-of-record reconciliation for Bassner v6, whose analytic sample is now 281, and orderly human consolidation of the open draft evidence stack.

## Next synthesis threshold

Do not strengthen [C002] beyond `provisional` until the review completes reproducible database/citation searches, duplicate formal risk-of-bias assessment, and at least one preregistered current-system randomized replication that isolates the relevant interaction policy and measures delayed novel independent transfer.
