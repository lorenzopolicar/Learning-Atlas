# S002 — AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning

## Why it matters

It tests a deliberately structured AI tutor in an authentic university course rather than treating an unprompted chatbot as the intervention.

## Identity and provenance

- Version re-inspected: complete ten-page *Scientific Reports* article on 19 October 2026.
- Study period: Fall 2023; the tutor used GPT-4 with exact snapshot unreported.
- Retrieval: publisher PDF SHA-256 `afa6965dfd885fccdeb7ba48bec6a9e8293886db12a02860bbc388fa93e7ff72`.
- Technology boundary: historical-system evidence for effect size; the prompt, scaffold and expert-authored-solution design remain mechanism-relevant.

## Study and findings

In a randomized controlled trial with 194 students in an undergraduate physics course, a carefully designed AI tutoring lesson was compared with in-class active learning. Students in the AI condition learned more in less time and reported greater engagement and motivation. The tutor used research-based constraints and detailed instructional design.

## Limitations and boundary conditions

This was a single course, institution, set of lessons, and implementation. Outcomes were short-term and close to instruction. The study compares a particular tutor lesson with a particular active-learning lesson, not AI with all human teaching. The authors explicitly caution against universal extrapolation.
