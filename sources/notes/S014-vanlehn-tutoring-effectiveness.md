# S014 — Relative effectiveness of tutoring systems

## Why it matters

The review challenges the simplistic idea that human tutoring is an indivisible gold standard and asks which interaction granularity and feedback mechanisms account for tutoring effects.

## Review and findings

VanLehn compares effect sizes across human tutoring, intelligent tutoring systems, and other tutoring arrangements. Step-based intelligent tutoring systems can approach the effectiveness of human tutoring in the studied domains, suggesting that timely, task-linked interaction matters.

## Limitations and boundary conditions

The review predates large language models and draws heavily from structured STEM problem-solving systems. Effect estimates depend on comparison conditions, outcome alignment, study quality, and domain. Conversational fluency should not be assumed equivalent to step-level pedagogical effectiveness.
